    Geotechnical Instruments · WebGL / GLSL Physics

    The Geo Lab — Three Photorealistic WebGL Instruments

    The former grid of abstract canvas loops has been refactored down to three high-fidelity WebGL instruments grounded in real optics and geotechnical physics: birefringence under cross-polarized light, Boussinesq / Mohr-Coulomb stress tensors beneath a foundation, and seismic slope stability with live Factor-of-Safety dynamics. Each renderer is GPU-accelerated with custom GLSL shaders, runs only while scrolled into view, scales quality to your hardware, and falls back to a static render where WebGL is unavailable.

    Optical Petrographic Thin-Section — Cross-Polarized Light

    A 30 µm slice of granitic rock under the petrographic microscope. Grains of quartz, plagioclase, biotite and olivine flash their Michel-Lévy interference colours as the rotatable polarizers sweep through extinction — computed per pixel in GLSL from real birefringence (Δn) values.

    Subsurface Strata & Geotechnical Load Stress Tensor

    A photorealistic 3D terrain block — regolith over schist over gneissic bedrock, built from procedural heightmaps, normal maps and PBR rock textures. A foundation pad applies load; the bulb of transmitted stress follows Boussinesq's elastic solution, coloured by the Mohr-Coulomb shear ratio, with effective stress σ' = σ − u responding to pore water pressure in real time.

    Active Slope Failure & Seismic Rupture Dynamics

    A Himalayan rock slope (Lesser Himalayan Gneiss, after the Tipura-Jalthan type landslide) dissected by a relict joint at dip β. Pseudo-static seismic loading raises the driving share of gravity; when the dynamic Factor of Safety drops below 1.0, a particle-based debris avalanche propagates along the shear plane.
